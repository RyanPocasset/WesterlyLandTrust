How these were made

Locations of possible cultural stone features are withheld from this public version. Boulders, boulder fields and ledges are natural landforms mapped from 2022 LiDAR and are unverified.
Stone piles, boulders and ledges here are LiDAR shapes that fit the criteria. None are verified. Treat locations as sensitive and share only with Tribal reviewers until they are field-checked.

Stone piles are screened toward small, hand-built piles of the kind the Makiawisug could have made, in places colonial farming didn't touch, tied to deep geology. A pile must pass all of these: 0.3–0.9 m tall and no more than about 4 m across. A dome profile rather than a steep-sided boulder. At least 10 m from any mapped stone wall. Not in a 1939 field on plowable soil; rocky and very stony ground counts as never-plowed. Within 75 m of a bedrock ledge, within 50 m of a large erratic, in a boulder field, or on shallow-bedrock soil. That cut 1,347 pile-shaped mounds to 113. High confidence means at least 0.45 m tall, standing apart from other rocks, and within 50 m of a ledge or erratic. LiDAR can't date a pile or tell a small boulder from a pile; field checks decide.

Significant boulders are at least 2 m tall with their top at least 1.5 m above 95% of the ground around them. Boulders about 1.9 m and taller also count if they stand 1.2 m clear and away from ledge faces. That excludes bedrock knobs. Boulder fields are areas with 15 or more rocks ≥0.5 m per hectare (at least 8 rocks), each described by soil, glacial deposit and bedrock unit.

Ledges are faces steeper than 45° with at least 2 m of relief on till or bedrock soils. Rockshelter settings are faces at least 3 m high, facing east to southwest, with level ground at the foot. Solar alignment assumes a flat horizon at 41.35°N: sunrise 57.5° (summer solstice), 89.5° (equinox), 122.5° (winter solstice).

1939 cover comes from canopy texture in the May 1939 photos, corrected frame by frame. Historic grassland is ground that was open in 1939 on droughty, excessively drained soils, the little-bluestem sandplain setting; confidence is High when it was still unforested in 1951–52. Conifers in 1962 come from the Feb–Apr 1962 leaf-off flight: canopy at least 1 SD darker than other woods in the same frame; today's conifers turn up there at 1.9× the rate of other 1962 woods.

Not covered: Camp Quequatuck (4.6 ac, acquired Dec 2025) has no public parcel boundary yet. Base imagery is RIGIS; the Connecticut side of the Pawcatuck is outside its coverage.
